Bag 1 · The story

It writes the story.

Then it plans the film like a set of building instructions.

You give it one line, who the story is for and what kind of story it is. A language model writes it, then plans it page by page: each page is a paragraph, and each step on the page is one picture, with who is in it and what we see.

For grown-ups The whole story is written before a single picture is drawn. Read it, change any line, and only then make the film.

The story is written in its own language. The notes for the pictures stay in English, which picture models follow best.

Bag 2 · The voice

It reads it out loud.

The voice comes first, and the pictures wait for it.

The story is narrated before anything is drawn, so every picture stays on screen exactly as long as its words take to say. Spanish is read a little slower than English, so in Spanish the pictures stay a little longer. Switch the language and watch the bricks grow.

For grown-ups In the app a voice model narrates, and you choose the narrator's voice. On this page your browser reads with its own.

    Bag 3 · The pictures

    It draws every picture.

    With the same friends in each one.

    An image model draws a cast sheet first, every character once. Then it draws each picture from the portraits of the characters in it, so the little red brick is the same brick from the first picture to the last.

    A picture starts as a jumble of colours, and the model finds the picture in it, a little clearer at every step.

    • Watercolour
    • Paper cut-out

    • Ink and pencil
    • Anime
    • 3D film

    For grown-ups Don't like a picture? Draw it again. The others stay exactly as they are, and only that one picture is made again.

      Bag 4 · The motion

      You choose what moves.

      Twelve pictures make one second of film.

      Try it: every press takes one picture, and your hero moves on a little in between. That is how films with toy bricks are made by hand.

      In the app nobody has to. A video model brings to life the pictures you mark; the others stay as pictures, with a slow camera move across them, which costs less.

      For grown-ups Before anything runs, Lanternist shows what the film will cost. Moving pictures cost more, so you choose where they matter.

          Bag 5 · The film

          Then it all clicks together.

          The pictures The voice The sounds The words at the bottom

          The voice goes over the pictures, and each moving picture keeps its own sound underneath. Inside a paragraph the pictures cut; between paragraphs one melts into the next. The words go on, and the sound is set so it's comfortable on a phone.

            For grown-ups

            The small print on the side of the box, in big letters.

            Made for their age
            You choose who the story is for. For toddlers: very simple words, gentle repetition and nothing scary at all. For ages 5 to 8: mild peril at most, and always a kind ending.
            You read it first
            The whole story is written before a single picture is drawn. Read it, change any line, then make the film. Change one scene later and only that scene is made again.
            Bedtime, winding down
            A bedtime story slows down in its last third and winds gently toward sleep.
            In your language
            Stories are written and read aloud in English, Spanish, Portuguese, French, German, Italian, Russian, Chinese, Japanese and Korean, with the words on screen.
            The price first
            You buy credits. Before anything runs, Lanternist shows what the film will cost, and afterwards it charges only for the steps that ran.
            Nothing to install
            A phone, a tablet or a computer is enough. Lanternist runs the models for you: there's no graphics card or keys to bring.
