A magic lantern for your stories

Give it one line. It puts on the show.

Lanternist turns an idea into a narrated story film. It writes the story, reads it aloud in your language, draws every scene with the same cast, and sets the moments you choose in motion.

Tonight's programme

Five reels on how Luna's film was made, and then the film.

Reel I

The writer

Every film starts as one line.

You give an idea, an audience and a length. The writer turns it into a story, then into a storyboard: who is in it, where it happens, and a list of shots. It cuts each paragraph into one to three shots on its sentences, the way a film editor would: a wide shot, then a close-up.

The narration is in the story's language. The picture prompts stay in English, which image models follow best. Change the language and watch.

Reel II

The narrator

The voice comes first.

Narration is recorded before anything is drawn, so each picture stays on screen exactly as long as its words. Spanish is read a little slower than English, and the shots stretch to fit.

Pick the narrator's voice from its presets, in any of the ten languages.

Reel III

The cast

Everyone looks like themselves, every time.

Lanternist draws a cast sheet first, then each character alone. Every picture is drawn from the portraits of only the characters in it, so Luna keeps her grey coat from the first shot to the last, and nobody wanders into a scene they aren't in.

Don't like a picture? Draw it again. The other pictures stay as they are.

Point at a shot, or tap it, to see who is in it.

    Reel IV

    The motion

    You choose which scenes move.

    Mark the shots that should come alive, and a video model animates them. The rest stay pictures, with a slow camera move across them. Video costs more than a still, so you spend it where it matters.

    Your choices carry into the show below.

      Reel V

      The mix

      Then the film is put together.

      The narration goes over the pictures, and each video keeps its own sound, set under the voice. Paragraphs dissolve into each other; the shots inside a paragraph cut. Subtitles go on, and the whole film is levelled to −16 LUFS, a comfortable loudness for a phone.

      Change one scene later and only that scene and the final mix are made again. Everything else comes from the cache, so the second render takes moments.

        The lanternist's notes

        What do I need?
        A phone or a computer. Lanternist runs the models for you, so there's nothing to install and no graphics card or API keys to bring.
        What does a film cost?
        You buy credits. Before anything runs, Lanternist shows what the film will cost, and afterwards it charges only for the steps that ran.
        Can I change things?
        Every line of the script, every picture, which shots move, the narrator's voice and the style. Changing one scene makes only that scene again.
        Which languages?
        Stories are written and narrated in English, Spanish, Portuguese, French, German, Italian, Russian, Chinese, Japanese and Korean.
